For colleagues and friends

Someone you know was laid off?

Here's what actually helps. Most of it takes less than half an hour, and none of it needs the perfect words.

Seven things that help

  1. Write their LinkedIn recommendation now. Don't wait to be asked. Two or three sentences about one piece of work you saw them do is plenty. It sits on their profile while recruiters are looking. Their profile → More → Recommend.
  2. Send them their numbers. Once the laptop's gone, so is the record of what they achieved. If you remember a result (the launch they ran, the backlog they cleared, the customer they kept), message it to them for their CV. Numbers and memories only, never company files.
  3. Make one introduction. Ask where they'd like to work next. Search each company on LinkedIn, filter people by your 1st connections, and ask that person first whether they'd take a 15-minute call. One warm intro beats ten job links.
  4. Refer them where you work. If there's a role that fits, an employee referral usually means a person reads the CV. Ask your recruiter how to submit one, and tell them you have.
  5. Offer one specific thing, with a day. An open offer of help leaves them to work out what to ask for. Offer a practice interview on Thursday, or lunch next week, and make it easy to say no.
  6. Check in at week three. Most messages arrive in the first few days, then stop, right when the job search gets long.
  7. Keep inviting them. To the lunch, the drinks, the group chat. Losing work friends is part of losing a job; it doesn't have to be.
Better to skip
  • Asking what happened, especially in a group. They'll tell you if they want to.
  • A stack of job links with no context. One role you know something about is worth more.
  • Looking for the silver lining. Leave that for them to find, if they want to.
