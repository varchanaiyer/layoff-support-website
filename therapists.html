Regular support you can afford

Cheapest first. Most people can get at least one of these for free or close to it.

  1. 1

    Your old job's counselling line (EAP)

    Many employers' Employee Assistance Programmes keep offering free, confidential counselling sessions for weeks or months after a layoff.

    Cost
    Free
    Good to know
    It varies by employer. Check your separation paperwork, or ask HR when your access ends.
    How to ask →
  2. 2

    Medicaid

    If your income has dropped, Medicaid may cover regular therapy at little or no cost. You can apply any time.

    Cost
    Usually free
    Good to know
    Some states cover few adults without children. Our health page explains the rules.
    Check if you qualify →
  3. 3

    Community health centers

    Federally funded clinics, many with counselling. They can't turn you away for not being able to pay.

    Cost
    Sliding fee based on income
    Good to know
    Counselling and waiting times vary by clinic, so call first.
    Find one near you →
  4. 4

    University training clinics

    Weekly therapy with trainee therapists supervised by licensed psychologists. Often the cheapest regular therapy around.

    Cost
    Sliding scale, for example $24 to $70 a session
    Good to know
    Often waiting lists, and therapists change each academic year.
    Search near you →
  5. 5

    Open Path Collective

    A national network of private therapists who see clients at a reduced rate. Good if you earn too much for a sliding scale.

    Cost
    $65 once, then $40 to $70 a session ($30 with a trainee)
    Good to know
    Only pay the $65 once you've found a therapist with space.
    Search Open Path →

How to use your old job's EAP

Look for "Employee Assistance Program" in your separation paperwork or benefits portal, or email HR: "Can I still use the EAP, and until when?" The counsellors don't tell your employer you called.

Asking for a lower fee is normal

Many therapists keep a few reduced-fee places and won't mention them unless you ask. Copy this, change the amount, and send it.

Hi, I was recently laid off and I'm looking for a therapist. Do you offer a sliding scale or reduced fee? I could manage about $[amount] a session. Thank you.

What to look for in a therapist right now

  • Ask about job loss and grief. Losing a job can feel like losing part of who you are. Not every therapist frames it that way.
  • Don't wait for the perfect fit. One conversation this week beats searching for three.
  • It's okay to switch. If it doesn't feel right after two or three sessions, try someone else.

Skip these for now

  • BetterHelp. About $260 to $400 a month, and the US Federal Trade Commission fined it in 2023 for sharing users' mental-health data with advertisers.
  • Subscription therapy apps you can't afford next month. Choose something you can keep going with.
