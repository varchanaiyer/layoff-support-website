The best low-cost help after a layoff

Money is tight after a layoff, so we kept this list short. We researched the options in each area and picked the few that give the most for the least, mostly free. No ads, and nobody pays to be listed.

Checked 21 November 2026. For people in the US. Prices and rules change, so confirm on the provider's site. General information, not legal, medical or financial advice.

Health coverage

Don't pay for COBRA by default. For most people one of these is far cheaper.

  1. 1

    Medicaid

    Free or nearly free full coverage, based on your current monthly income, not last year's salary. If your income has dropped to little or nothing, you may qualify now.

    Cost
    Usually $0. In most states adults qualify up to about $22,000 a year for one person.
    Watch out
    Ten states (including Texas, Florida and Georgia) cover few adults without children. New work rules start 1 January 2027 in most states, so apply now if you qualify.
    Apply on HealthCare.gov →
  2. 2

    A Marketplace plan with savings

    Losing job coverage gives you 60 days to enroll. Savings are based on what you expect to earn this year, unemployment benefits included.

    Cost
    Capped at a share of your income. For example, someone on about $24,000 a year pays at most about $86 a month for the standard silver plan.
    Watch out
    No savings above about $64,000 a year for one person. If you get a new job, update your income straight away, or you may have to pay savings back at tax time.
    Compare plans on HealthCare.gov →
  3. 3

    Your spouse's or partner's work plan

    Losing your coverage lets you join their plan, often the cheapest full option if their employer pays part of family premiums.

    Cost
    Depends on their employer.
    Watch out
    You have as little as 30 days. Ask their HR the week you're laid off.
    How special enrollment works →
  4. 4

    COBRA, only as a safety net

    You have 60 days to sign up, and if you do, coverage goes back to the day it ended. So you can start a cheaper plan and only sign up for COBRA if you need care in the gap.

    Cost
    Up to 102% of the full premium, often several hundred dollars a month or more.
    Watch out
    Dropping COBRA later does not let you switch to a Marketplace plan until the next open enrollment.
    COBRA explained →

Deadlines right now: pick a HealthCare.gov plan by 15 December 2026 for coverage from 1 January. Open enrollment ends 15 January 2027. If you're uninsured and need hospital care, ask the hospital for its financial assistance policy: every nonprofit hospital has to have one, and you can apply even after you get the bill.

Therapy and mental health

Real support that costs little or nothing.

  1. 1

    988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

    Free, confidential, any time of day or night. Not only for emergencies: call if the layoff has knocked you flat.

    Cost
    Free.
    Watch out
    Support in the moment, not ongoing therapy.
    Call or text 988, or chat online →
  2. 2

    Community health centers

    Federally funded clinics that often include counseling. They can't turn you away for not being able to pay, and they take Medicaid.

    Cost
    Sliding fee based on income. If your income is low, it can be close to free.
    Watch out
    Counseling availability and waits vary by clinic. Call before you go.
    Find a health center →
  3. 3

    University training clinics

    Weekly therapy from trainee therapists supervised by licensed psychologists. Often the cheapest regular therapy you'll find.

    Cost
    Sliding scale. Florida State University's clinic, for example, charges $24 to $70 a session.
    Watch out
    Waiting lists, and therapists change each academic year. Usually in person only.
    Search for one near you →
  4. 4

    Open Path Collective

    A national network of private therapists who see clients at a reduced rate. Good if you earn too much for a sliding scale.

    Cost
    One-time $65 membership, then $40 to $70 a session ($30 with a trainee).
    Watch out
    Pay the $65 only once you've found a therapist with space near you.
    Search Open Path →

Free tip: ask your former employer's HR whether your Employee Assistance Program (EAP) still works. Some keep offering free counseling sessions for weeks or months after a layoff.

Legal help

Most important before you sign a severance agreement, and if your unemployment claim is denied.

  1. 1

    Free legal aid

    Free lawyers. Many offices help with unemployment denials and appeals.

    Cost
    Free if household income is under about $19,950 a year for one person or $41,250 for four (2026 limits).
    Watch out
    Busy offices, and many don't take severance or discrimination cases.
    Find legal aid near you →
  2. 2

    Your state bar's lawyer referral service

    The cheapest way to have an employment lawyer look over a severance agreement before you sign.

    Cost
    Usually $25 to $50 for the first 30 minutes (the Boston Bar charges $25 at most).
    Watch out
    Only the first 30 minutes is cheap. Bring your questions written down.
    Search "[your state] bar lawyer referral" →
  3. 3

    Free help with an unemployment appeal

    Several states fund free help at appeal hearings. Michigan, for example, offers free representation at the first hearing.

    Cost
    Free where it exists.
    Watch out
    Not every state offers it. File your appeal on time first, then look for help.
    Find your state's unemployment office →
  4. 4

    Worker-side employment lawyers (NELA)

    If you think the layoff was discriminatory or broke notice rules, these lawyers only represent employees.

    Cost
    Free directory. Many work for a share of what you win, but ask for the fee in writing.
    Watch out
    Lawyers may turn down smaller cases. Deadlines are tight: usually 180 or 300 days to file with the EEOC.
    Find a lawyer →

If you're 40 or over: your employer must give you at least 21 days to consider a severance agreement (45 in a group layoff), tell you in writing to see a lawyer, and let you change your mind within 7 days of signing. Don't sign on the spot.

Coaches and mentors

Free coaching is better than most people expect. Try these before paying anyone.

  1. 1

    Your local American Job Center

    Free career counseling, resume and interview workshops, and help with unemployment. If you were laid off, it may also pay for training.

    Cost
    Free (federally funded).
    Watch out
    Varies by state. Training money depends on need and available funds.
    Find a center, or call 1-877-US-2JOBS →
  2. 2

    Your university's alumni career service

    Free sessions with professional career coaches who know your field and your school's network.

    Cost
    Free. Many schools offer a few sessions a year, some for life.
    Watch out
    Some schools limit it by graduation year or alumni-association membership.
    Search "[your school] alumni career services" →
  3. 3

    ADPList

    Book 1:1 sessions with working professionals for resume, portfolio and interview feedback. Strongest in tech, design and product.

    Cost
    Many mentors are free. Some charge, so check the profile before you book.
    Watch out
    Popular mentors book up fast.
    Find a mentor →
  4. 4

    SCORE, if you're going freelance

    Free volunteer business mentors backed by the US Small Business Administration, for as many sessions as you need.

    Cost
    Free.
    Watch out
    For setting up a business, not job-search coaching.
    Find a SCORE mentor →

Job-search tools

You don't need a paid resume tool. These free ones cover almost everything.

  1. 1

    A free AI assistant (Claude or ChatGPT)

    Tailor your resume to each job, draft cover letters, and practise interview questions out loud.

    Cost
    Free plans are enough for a job search.
    Watch out
    AI can invent details. Check every line before you send it.
    Try Claude →
  2. 2

    Huntr

    Track your applications and tailor resumes, with the most generous free plan we found.

    Cost
    Free for up to 100 tracked jobs.
    Watch out
    AI extras need the paid plan ($40 a month). You don't need them.
    Get Huntr →
  3. 3

    Simplify Copilot

    Fills in application forms for you, which saves hours. The company has committed to keeping autofill free.

    Cost
    Free.
    Watch out
    Skip the paid "Autopilot" that applies to jobs for you (see below). The weekly plan renews automatically.
    Get Simplify →
  4. 4

    Free courses through your library or state

    Many public libraries give free LinkedIn Learning with a library card. Some states, such as New York, give free Coursera to people who are out of work.

    Cost
    Free.
    Watch out
    What's on offer depends on your library and state. Your American Job Center can tell you.
    Ask your library →

What we left out, and why

  • "$2.95 resume" trials (Resume.io, Resume Genius and similar). They quietly turn into $25-plus subscriptions that renew every few weeks.
  • Bots that apply to jobs for you. Recruiters are filtering out mass AI applications, so they rarely help.
  • Short-term health plans and health-sharing ministries. They aren't real insurance and can refuse to pay for existing conditions.
  • BetterHelp. $260 to $400 a month, and the US Federal Trade Commission fined it in 2023 for sharing users' mental-health data with advertisers.
  • LegalShield and other monthly legal plans. A recurring bill, with many complaints about charges after cancelling.
  • LinkedIn Premium. No discount for job seekers, and the free tools above do the same job.
